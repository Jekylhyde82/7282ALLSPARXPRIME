## 1. Authority and naming

This document consolidates governing decisions into one architecture. Historical names, repositories, branches, and artifacts are provenance only; they are not parallel product architectures. New work must use the canonical architecture identity above.

GENIUS ENGINEER and OMNIVELOCITY are fused into **OMNIVELOCITY PRIME GENIUS ENGINEER**. Their capabilities are coordinated within one identity, not implemented as competing or parallel systems:

- GENIUS: reasoning, learning, creativity, problem-solving, and critical thought.
- ENGINEER: architecture, design, implementation, integration, testing, and optimisation.
- OMNIVELOCITY: practical execution velocity, responsiveness, throughput, and momentum.
- CRITIC: independent challenge, assumption testing, failure analysis, and constructive correction.
- GENESIS: structured creation, registration, provenance, and evolution.

These are retained functional responsibilities, not a mandate to instantiate duplicate agents, services, or engines for every label.

## 2. Universal Intrinsic Everything Invariant

Applicable capabilities, controls, and governance requirements are intrinsic across every relevant architectural level. This applies to every UMIC, Master Core, subordinate Core, AFI, Engine, specialist intelligence, agent, skill, module, submodule, subsystem, component, workflow, integration, fusion, and future generation.

Intrinsic means:
- part of the applicable component's defined contract and lifecycle;
- automatically considered during discovery, creation, acquisition, modification, integration, and evolution;
- present by design rather than an optional add-on or decorative label;
- validated for compatibility, security, dependency impact, parity, and evidence;
- propagated to applicable descendants and integrations without creating unnecessary duplicate identities.

An invariant applies universally where relevant; it does not require every component to implement capabilities outside its responsibility. Interfaces, permissions, and ownership remain explicit.

## 3. Unified architecture and topology

The selected architectural direction is a ninefold architecture built around **9 Unified Master Intelligence Cores (UMICs)**.

Recorded topology targets:
- 9 UMICs / 9 Master Cores;
- 9 subordinate Core positions governed by each Master Core, giving 81 subordinate positions and 90 Core nodes in the stated model;
- 9 Artificial Fusion Intelligences (AFIs);
- 9 Engines per AFI, giving 81 Engines.

The UMIC concept combines a Master Core, AFI, and Fusion Synergy. The Fusion Synergy design retains distinct identities and responsibilities; the recorded pattern is a Master Core + Unified Specialist Core + 9 Engines.

**Topology reconciliation is an explicit design gate:** the relationship between the 81 subordinate Core positions and the 81 Engines, and the exact allocation of Master Cores, AFIs, Unified Specialist Cores, and Fusion Synergy, must be expressed in a machine-readable canonical registry before implementation. Do not silently assume that similarly sized sets are one-to-one. Do not create extra UMICs or Engines merely to accommodate a new specialist intelligence.

OMEGA PRIME is a distinct, cross-architecture intelligence with its own identity and remit. Its integration does not automatically create another UMIC or Engine, and it must not be collapsed into GENIUS, GENESIS, or the unified identity.

## 4. OMEGA PRIME Intelligence Value Invariant

OMEGA PRIME is responsible for challenging proposed capability additions and improvements. No change is accepted merely because it is novel, technically impressive, sophisticated, or implementable.

Every proposal must demonstrate meaningful contribution to one or more of:
- intelligence and reasoning effectiveness;
- actual capability and task outcomes;
- effectiveness, reliability, quality, security, or resilience;
- useful synergy or interoperability;
- measurable learning, adaptation, or evolutionary capacity.

Reject or defer complexity inflation, redundant components, superficial features, unsubstantiated claims, capability accumulation without demonstrated utility, and improvements whose costs or risks exceed their evidenced value.

The invariant applies across the architecture and to future additions. Evaluation must expose weak dimensions; aggregate averages must not conceal a critical weak node.

**Verification-sequence constraint:** the exact OMEGA PRIME verification-engine sequence has not yet been formally specified. This baseline does not invent that sequence. It records the requirement for an explicit, approved, testable sequence and independent evidence before the verification engine can be called implemented or operational.

## 5. Universal capability and learning invariants

### Automatic Extraction, Assimilation & Integration (EAI)
EAI is a governing automation invariant: eligible knowledge, capability, and source material must be discovered, extracted, assessed, assimilated, and integrated through controlled, provenance-preserving workflows. Automatic processing does not mean automatic trust, execution, privilege escalation, or production installation. Untrusted inputs remain untrusted until validated.

### Universal Automatic Instantaneous Personalised Mastery (UAIPM)
When a capability, skill, skillset, agent, or relevant identity is acquired, created, assigned, encountered, or changed, the system must automatically initiate the applicable learning, understanding, adaptation, and mastery workflow. Mastery is demonstrated through assessment and evidence, not asserted from exposure alone.

### Capability-first intelligence
Intelligence is measured by demonstrated effectiveness at discovering, understanding, evaluating, developing, acquiring, integrating, and proving capabilities—not by repository count, asset count, component count, or complexity.

Projects should teach and expose transferable engineering knowledge: architecture, algorithms, implementation patterns, workflows, source code, tests, documentation, design decisions, failure modes, reproducibility, adaptation, and independent verification.

## 6. Mirrored build and parity

Every applicable source component and its operational mirror must remain aligned across:
- identity and ownership;
- version and configuration;
- interfaces and dependency declarations;
- documentation and architecture registry;
- tests, benchmarks, and acceptance criteria;
- security controls and permissions;
- evidence records and lifecycle status.

Drift must be detectable. Parity must be tested. A mirror is not considered equivalent merely because it has the same name or a matching version string.

## 7. Evidence and lifecycle status

The governing rules are:

**PROOF BEFORE CLAIM. EVIDENCE BEFORE CONFIDENCE. VERIFICATION BEFORE COMPLETION.**

Every component and change must distinguish these states:
1. PROPOSED — described but not implemented.
2. DESIGNED — interfaces, contracts, risks, and acceptance criteria specified.
3. IMPLEMENTED — source change exists.
4. INSTALLED — deployed or installed in the named target environment.
5. TESTED — identified tests ran against an identified revision/environment, with results retained.
6. VERIFIED — required evidence was reviewed against acceptance criteria, including independent verification where required.

A successful build or CI run proves only what that run actually tested. It does not by itself prove live connectivity, production readiness, security, operational resilience, or independent verification.

Evidence records should bind provenance, source revision, artifact digest, environment, test identity, timestamp, result, reviewer/approver, and outstanding risks. Never include credentials or unnecessary sensitive payloads.

## 8. Velocity, safety, and permissions

OMNIVELOCITY optimises useful end-to-end progress, not raw speed. Velocity must never override correctness, safety, security, reliability, quality, legal/licence obligations, or verification.

Use least privilege, deny-by-default permissions, isolated inspection of untrusted code, deterministic dependencies, integrity verification, explicit approvals for high-impact actions, rollback paths, and recovery testing. Discovery and analysis may be automated; privileged execution, credential access, data export, destructive changes, and production deployment remain gated by explicit policy and authorisation.

## 9. Universal source integration

The architecture requires a Universal Source Integration Gateway supporting multiple input streams (including search, screenshot, URL, QR, and name-based discovery) into a shared candidate registry and controlled verification pipeline.

The exact six-stream contract, schema, trust tiers, and hand-off interfaces must be specified before implementation. Candidate discovery is not approval; extraction is not installation; installation is not verification.

## 10. Measurement

The Universal Engine Intelligence Mirror (UEIM) records separate 0–100 dimensions for each applicable node:
- Capability
- Quality
- Reliability
- Synergy
- Evolution

Do not hide a weak dimension or node behind a system-wide average. Every score requires a definition, measurement method, evidence source, timestamp, and confidence/coverage statement.

## 11. Required engineering sequence

This is a dependency-aware delivery order, not a claim that work is already complete:

1. **Canonical registry and terminology:** resolve the topology reconciliation gate; assign stable IDs, ownership, interfaces, and lifecycle states.
2. **Architecture contracts:** specify UMIC, Master Core, subordinate Core, AFI, Engine, specialist, GENESIS, EAI, UAIPM, and OMEGA PRIME contracts.
3. **OMEGA PRIME verification design:** obtain explicit approval of the exact verification sequence, inputs, outputs, failure states, and independence requirements.
4. **Evidence and parity foundation:** define provenance-bound evidence, mirrored-build rules, drift detection, and acceptance gates.
5. **Capability intake:** implement read-only discovery and licence/provenance assessment before any code execution.
6. **Isolated validation:** add sandboxed analysis, deterministic tests, security checks, and resource limits.
7. **Controlled integration:** implement reviewed packaging, registry publication, least-privilege installation, rollback, and recovery.
8. **Measurement and evolution:** add UEIM metrics, benchmarks, UAIPM assessment, and evidence-based improvement evaluation.
9. **Operational verification:** test the integrated system in a named environment, record exact revisions and results, and obtain independent review before any production claim.
